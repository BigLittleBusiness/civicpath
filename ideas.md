# CivicPath Marketing Site — Design Direction

## Three initial visual approaches

### Approach 1

**Theme Name:** Civic Field Manual

**Very Brief Intro:** A quiet, confident editorial system that feels like a well-used regional planning field guide. It treats clarity, evidence and stewardship as visual assets rather than using generic software theatrics.

**Probability:** 0.07

### Approach 2

**Theme Name:** Horizon Ledger

**Very Brief Intro:** A warm landscape-inspired direction using broad bands, practical data marks and an optimistic horizon motif. It makes regional growth feel tangible and place-based.

**Probability:** 0.04

## Chosen approach: Civic Field Manual

### Design Movement

**Contemporary editorial civic design**, informed by regional planning documents, Australian field guides and high-quality public-sector reports. The site should feel considered and operational, not like a venture-capital startup template.

### Core Principles

1. **Earn trust through restraint.** Use direct language, deliberate spacing and practical proof points rather than hyperbole, excessive gradients or decorative noise.
2. **Make complex work legible.** Treat the platform narrative as a clear route: priority, project, readiness, funding pathway and delivery.
3. **Respect the regional context.** Use grounded natural tones, broad horizons and human-scale language without resorting to stock rural imagery or nostalgia.
4. **Design for decision-makers.** Every section must answer a council buyer’s implicit question: what problem does this solve, how does it fit our systems, and what is the next low-risk step?

### Colour Philosophy

The palette starts with a deep **river slate** to communicate governance, trust and operational focus. A muted **eucalypt green** supplies the ownable product signal and connects to regional landscapes without becoming an environmental brand. Warm **sandstone** and **paper** surfaces create the feeling of a practical field manual, while a restrained **apricot marker** is reserved for decisions, deadlines and calls to action. This avoids both corporate blue monotony and overused high-tech purple.

### Layout Paradigm

Use a **route-and-margin** structure rather than a conventional centred-grid landing page. Major sections move down a single left-aligned editorial spine, with a vertical route line and numbered waypoints on wide screens. Content alternates between a wide primary narrative column and a supporting evidence column, echoing a working project brief. On mobile, the route line becomes a compact section marker.

### Signature Elements

1. **The Gate:** The supplied open-gateway mark is used as the provisional identity, signalling a practical path forward.
2. **Route markers:** Small numbered circles and connecting lines frame the strategic workflow throughout the page.
3. **Field notes:** Short, high-value statements set in a contrasting serif italic to give the site a considered editorial rhythm.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should be quiet and useful. Links, accordions and cards should clearly reveal affordance through a modest colour shift and underline treatment. Calls to action should guide a reader toward a low-risk conversation, never pressure them into a product commitment.

### Animation

Use only brief, subtle reveal motion for in-flow sections and route markers, respecting reduced-motion preferences. No looping decorative motion, parallax, glowing effects or animated gradients. Hover states may shift a border or underline by a few pixels to clarify interactivity.

### Typography System

Use **DM Sans** for interface and body copy because of its compact clarity, paired with **Source Serif 4** for editorial pull quotes, numeric callouts and select section leads. Headings use DM Sans in a firm 600–700 weight; body copy stays at 400–500. The wordmark should use a customised DM Sans treatment with measured letter spacing rather than a default font-only lockup.

### Brand Essence

**CivicPath helps regional councils turn strategic priorities into delivery-ready projects with a clear route to action.**

Personality: **grounded, capable, clear.**

### Brand Voice

Headlines are direct, specific and calm. CTAs sound like an invitation to reduce uncertainty, not an urgent software sale.

Example lines:

> “Know which projects are ready to move — and what is holding the rest back.”

> “Start with one live portfolio. Build a clearer path from there.”

### Wordmark & Logo

Use the supplied **Gate** mark as a provisional symbol. Pair it with a confident lower-case/Title Case CivicPath wordmark, maintaining enough clear space for the icon to remain legible at favicon and app-button sizes. The gate is a placeholder rather than a final trade-mark-ready asset.

### Signature Brand Color

**Eucalypt Signal — #0F7B61.** This is the ownable accent colour for the Gate, key route markers and action links.

## Style Decisions

- **Display typography:** DM Sans headlines prioritise civic clarity and breathing room over compressed, trend-led letter spacing. Source Serif 4 appears consistently in field notes, route numbers and reflective proof statements.
- **Route continuity:** Every major chapter of the page carries a visible route marker or field-note annotation so the narrative reads as one deliberate pathway, not separate landing-page blocks.
- **Signature colour rule:** Eucalypt Signal is the primary Gate, route and action colour. Apricot is reserved for decisions, readiness blockers and deadline annotations; it is never general decoration.
